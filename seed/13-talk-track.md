## 1. Slide-by-Slide Stage Talk Track

### Slide 1 — Title
* **What you say (max 20s):** "I'm KJ Cornish, and we're building Animal Scrypt in Savannah. You collect an animal that remembers you, and it can grow up into a worker. If someone copies the worker you trained, you still get paid."
* **Line you do NOT read off the slide:** Do not read "ANIMAL SCRYPT: Animals that remember you—and do a job."
* **Question you expect:** "Is this a toy company or an AI software company?"
* **Honest answer:** "It's one company—Robotic Discipline—where the physical shell is the front door to a persistent software worker."

### Slide 2 — Hold the Body
* **What you say (max 20s):** *(Hold up the shell)* "This is a 3D-printed shell with a QR and NFC tag in its base. When I scan it, it doesn't open a blank chat window—it opens this specific creature's workspace, biography, and memory bank."
* **Line you do NOT read off the slide:** "Hold the body. Scan the tag."
* **Question you expect:** "Are there motors or a battery inside that shell?"
* **Honest answer:** "Not in v1. Desktop robots like Living.AI EMO cost $279 and die in two hours. A passive tag never runs out of battery and costs us under eight dollars to print."

### Slide 3 — Chatbots Forget
* **What you say (max 20s):** "Right now, people pay ten dollars a month for Character.ai or twenty for Replika. Go read their subreddits today—the number one complaint is that the bot forgets your project and your name after twenty messages."
* **Line you do NOT read off the slide:** "$9.99/mo on Character.ai—and it still forgets last Tuesday."
* **Question you expect:** "Can't OpenAI or Character.ai just make their context window bigger?"
* **Honest answer:** "Bigger context windows just stuff more chat noise into the prompt. Our animal only keeps facts you explicitly click Approve to save."

### Slide 4 — Toys Do Nothing
* **What you say (max 20s):** "On the physical side, Pop Mart did over five billion dollars in revenue last year selling vinyl figures, and Tonies did 630 million euros selling NFC figures. People love physical characters on their desk, but right now those figures can't remember you or do a job."
* **Line you do NOT read off the slide:** "$5.1 Billion in Pop Mart figures sitting silent on desks."
* **Question you expect:** "Why do adults want a physical toy for a software agent?"
* **Honest answer:** "That is the exact hypothesis we are testing next week with three adults at SCAD. We think holding the character anchors the habit better than a browser bookmark."

### Slide 5 — Approve What It Remembers
* **What you say (max 20s):** "When you talk to your animal—whether its job is engineer, artist, musician, or researcher—it proposes a memory card. You click Approve, Edit, or Delete. Seven days later, even after a server restart, those approved facts are still there."
* **Line you do NOT read off the slide:** "1. Approve what it remembers. Engineer · Artist · Musician · Researcher."
* **Question you expect:** "What LLM are you running underneath?"
* **Honest answer:** "We don't train our own foundation model. The identity and approved memory sit in our SQLite and Supabase ledger so we can swap the model underneath anytime."

### Slide 6 — Train a Worker, Get Paid
* **What you say (max 20s):** "Once you feed your engineer animal twenty facts about your 3D-printer tolerances, a classmate can fork a child animal from your template—with your private notes stripped out. When they buy that child animal, twenty percent goes back to you."
* **Line you do NOT read off the slide:** "2. Train a worker. Get paid when it's copied."
* **Question you expect:** "How do you enforce the royalty—is this a crypto token?"
* **Honest answer:** "Long term, ownership and royalties settle on Litecoin rails. For our 8-week MVP, we are recording the split in a simulated test ledger first so we prove people actually want to fork a worker before touching live payments."

### Slide 7 — Art School Is Our Factory
* **What you say (max 20s):** "I'm a rising sophomore at SCAD. Instead of waiting six months for an overseas factory mold, I print the shells on campus, stick the tag in the base, and film the print for our distribution."
* **Line you do NOT read off the slide:** "3. Art school is our factory."
* **Question you expect:** "Does SCAD own your IP if you use campus printers?"
* **Honest answer:** "We don't know the exact line for commercial sales yet—checking SCAD's student IP policy with counsel and the SCAD Accelerator is on our legal checklist before we sell commercial batches."

### Slide 8 — What Is True Today
* **What you say (max 20s):** "Let me be completely straight about where we are: we have zero paid users today. What we have is our local memory and royalty kernel running in Python and SQLite, and a seven-point checklist for the next six weeks: one shell, seven-day recall, and three consenting adult testers."
* **Line you do NOT read off the slide:** The bullet list on Slide 8.
* **Question you expect:** "Why haven't you given it to three people already?"
* **Honest answer:** "Because before today I was spreading the idea across three different names and ten roadmap phases. We just locked the seven-box MVP to run that test this week."

### Slide 9 — Pricing Ladder
* **What you say (max 20s):** "We start at forty-five dollars for the first shell and nine dollars a month for memory so SCAD makers can buy it immediately. Once seven-day recall is proven, prices step up to 129 to 249 dollars for pro worker docks and guided learning kits."
* **Line you do NOT read off the slide:** "Starting at $45 + $9/mo."
* **Question you expect:** "What happened to the 300 founders at $2,500?"
* **Honest answer:** "That is a later cohort scenario, not our first checkout. Asking $2,500 before a single animal has survived a seven-day test is backwards."

### Slide 10 — Team & Ask
* **What you say (max 20s):** "I'm a U.S. veteran, a resident assistant at SCAD, and I also work on an equine AI sensing research thread on campus. I'm asking to join the SCAD Accelerator cohort to test our first ten paid shells and prepare for a pre-seed round."
* **Line you do NOT read off the slide:** Your bio lines on the slide.
* **Question you expect:** "Are you building this solo while taking a full course load?"
* **Honest answer:** "Yes—and per Julia's advice in the SCAD Accelerator session, I'm aligning my SCADpro and studio coursework directly with this build so every week compounds."

## 2. Timed Pitch Versions

### The Hallway Line (5 Seconds)
"You collect an animal that remembers you, and it can grow up into a worker. If someone copies it, the original maker still gets paid."

### The 30-Second Version
"I'm KJ Cornish at SCAD in Savannah, building Animal Scrypt. People spend ten dollars a month on Character.ai for bots that forget them after twenty messages, and five billion dollars a year on Pop Mart figures that sit on a desk doing nothing. We pair a 3D-printed animal shell with a browser workspace that only saves the project facts you approve—and remembers them seven days later. As you train your animal as an engineer, artist, or researcher, others can fork a child worker from it and you earn a twenty percent royalty. We have the memory kernel built and are testing our first three shells on campus this week."

### The 2-Minute Version
"I'm KJ Cornish, founder of Robotic Discipline, and our product is Animal Scrypt.
*(Hold up shell)* Imagine holding a 3D-printed creature on your desk. When you scan its tag, it opens a workspace in your browser. It has a name, a personality, and a job—engineer, artist, musician, or researcher.
Right now, if you use a chatbot like Character.ai or Replika, it either forgets your project specs by next week or hallucinates things you never said. And if you buy a collectible figure from Pop Mart or a Toniebox figurine, it can't think or work with you.
With Animal Scrypt, when you tell your creature something important about your project, it proposes a memory card. You click Approve. Seven days later—even after restarting the server—it still knows those exact facts.
Here is where the business compounds: once you've trained an engineer animal on your 3D-printing tolerances, a classmate can fork a Child Animal from your template—with your private notes stripped out. When they buy that child animal, our ledger credits you a twenty percent creator royalty.
Why am I the one building this? I'm a rising sophomore at SCAD, a U.S. military veteran on a diver path, and a resident assistant. I have an art school's 3D-printing labs right outside my door, and I also work on a SCADpro research thread studying longitudinal AI signals for real horses.
Here is what is true today: we have zero paid users. We have the local Python and SQLite memory and royalty kernel built. Over the next six weeks, our only job is to check seven boxes: one printed shell, seven-day recall of three approved facts, one simulated royalty transfer, and three consenting adults completing the test on campus.
We're starting at $45 per shell plus $9 a month for student makers, then growing our prices into $129 to $249 pro and learning kits as proof comes in."

### Recommended Structure: Hybrid PBC + Trust Majority + Nonprofit DAO Layer

A pure nonprofit cannot issue equity shares or let a founder own a majority stake in the commercial business. A pure DAO without a legal wrapper carries partnership liability risk. The practical solution used by many mission-driven and crypto-adjacent projects is a dual (or multi) entity hybrid.

\*\*1. Delaware Public Benefit Corporation (PBC) as the operating company\*\*  

- This is a for-profit corporation. It can issue equity shares, raise capital, pay people, distribute profits, and pursue exits or growth.

- Embed one or more specific public benefits in the certificate of incorporation, for example:

  - Reducing loneliness and supporting human connection through ethical AI companions.

  - Empowering families, neighborhoods, and homestead communities with specialized agents that foster collaboration, resilience, and shared memory.

  - Advancing responsible, human-centered AI that prioritizes user agency, privacy, and long-term well-being.

- Directors must balance shareholders’ financial interests, the interests of people materially affected by the company (users, families, communities), and the named public benefit(s). This gives legal cover for mission-aligned decisions.

- You can later pursue B Corp certification (the third-party B Lab badge) on top of the PBC legal form for the external signal. They are related but distinct: PBC is the legal entity; Certified B Corp is the voluntary assessment and brand mark.

- The PBC owns or licenses the commercial IP, runs the product, handles contracts, hiring, subscriptions, agent sales, physical goods, marketplace fees, and Litecoin integrations.

\*\*2. Founder’s trust holds the majority of voting shares\*\*  

- Form a trust (start with a revocable living trust for flexibility; consider irrevocable or directed/dynasty features later for stronger protection and estate planning). Delaware trust law is particularly flexible.

- The trust acquires the majority of the voting shares of the PBC at formation (commonly structured so the founder/trustee retains practical control, e.g., 60–80%+ voting power depending on your plans).

- You can still issue minority shares, options, or restricted stock to early supporters, team members, or investors without losing control.

- This supports your goal of “own a majority in my trust” while enabling equity grants. It also aids asset protection and family legacy planning.

- Later option (more advanced): layer a purpose trust or long-term benefit trust (similar to models used by Anthropic or the Patagonia approach) that holds some voting rights specifically to protect the mission, while you retain influence through trustees or advisory roles. Purpose trusts are stronger mission locks but are custom and more expensive—better after the company has traction.

\*\*3. Nonprofit DAO layer (Wyoming DUNA or foundation)\*\*  

- Separate nonprofit entity for community governance, open protocols, and public-goods work.

- Strong practical option: Wyoming DUNA (Decentralized Unincorporated Nonprofit Association). It gives limited liability, legal existence, blockchain-friendly governance, and a nonprofit purpose. Token holders can be members; profits cannot be distributed to members as profit but can support the mission (and reasonable compensation is allowed).

- Alternative or complementary: a foundation (Cayman, Swiss, or U.S. nonprofit depending on goals) that holds treasury or certain IP for grants and open elements.

- The DAO can govern specific things: open standards for agent memory, community treasury for grants (free specialized agents for nonprofits or underserved communities, research on companionship AI), certain marketplace rules, or evolution of open-source components.

- The PBC can hold some governance tokens initially and progressively decentralize. Clear inter-entity agreements (licenses, services, treasury flows) keep commercial operations in the PBC and community/public-goods work in the DAO.

This hybrid lets you issue shares and own a majority via the trust, embed a public benefit (B Corp / PBC orientation), and add a nonprofit DAO element without forcing the entire business into a pure nonprofit or pure unincorporated DAO.

### Key Caveats

- PBC status alone does not permanently lock the mission—it can be changed by stockholder vote unless you add stronger protections (supermajority, stockholder agreement, purpose trust, etc.).

- Document the balancing duty in major decisions.

- Tokens for the DAO should be structured carefully (utility/governance, not investment securities) with proper legal review.

- Tax treatment of the PBC, trust, and any nonprofit/DAO layer needs CPA advice.

- Progressive decentralization is more credible than claiming full decentralization on day one.

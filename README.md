## What is Robotic Discipline?

**Robotic Discipline** is a physical-digital AI companion and character computing company founded by Kendren "KJ" Cornish.

The central hypothesis of Robotic Discipline is that **a physical collectible character can serve as a recognizable, user-controlled entry point into a persistent AI computing workspace, low-pressure human connection, specialized tools, and an evolving fictional civilization.**

Rather than treating interactive collectibles, privacy-first local AI companions, character operating environments, and community robotics as separate startups, **Robotic Discipline** unifies the founder's entire body of work—previously explored under the names **MADKEN**, **Animal Scrypt**, **Disciple**, and **Space Fort**—into a single company, a single knowledge base, and a staged product roadmap.

---

## Three-Level Summary

* **For a 10-year-old:** Imagine owning a toy character that has its own little computer world inside a dock on your desk. When you tap or place your figure on the dock, its room opens up on your screen. You can talk to it, work on projects together, play games, and choose exactly which memories it keeps. Each character is good at different things—like building robots, practicing sports, or telling stories—and when two friends bring their characters together, they can team up.
* **For a 24-year-old:** Robotic Discipline builds collectible physical characters that unlock specialized, local-first AI workspaces and low-pressure social coordination. Instead of juggling generic chat windows or performative social media apps, you tap an NFC-paired character onto a desk dock to open a persistent project workspace (for CAD/engineering, study, creative work, or athletic tracking) with an inspectable memory ledger and optional "parallel presence" co-working with friends.
* **For a professor:** Robotic Discipline is an interdisciplinary human-computer interaction (HCI) and embodied agent architecture investigating tangible user interfaces (TUIs) as cryptographic and cognitive anchors for portable AI identity. It separates immutable authored character lore, user-approved episodic memory ledgers, and bounded tool-execution permissions across local/hybrid LLM runtimes, evaluating whether tangible embodiment and explicit consent gates improve user agency, task completion, and low-pressure social connection.
